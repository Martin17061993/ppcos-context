## Executive read

This is the highest score of the eleven audits run on this account today, and it is the most important one to read in context. **The offer is not the problem. It is, by some distance, the strongest asset in this business.**

The value case is genuinely hard to compete with: designs by named tattoo artists with verifiable Instagram handles, sold as "limited art pieces" — a competitor can match a 400GSM hoodie but cannot sell Rob Steele's artwork. Scarcity is authentic rather than manufactured (roughly 40% of the product feed is genuinely out of stock, and the shop demotes products below 10 units automatically). Trust is well built: 4.6/5 from 6,477 reviews, 30-day returns on top of the statutory 14, full German legal transparency, FairCommerce membership since 2018. Four named customer personas. The offer clears every ecommerce minimum on the checklist and scores 13 of 15.

**Which makes the session's central finding sharper, not softer.** Today's landing page audit scored 56% and found that punk, goth, metal and rocker appear **zero times** on the page the non-brand campaigns send traffic to. Read alongside this audit, that is not a content-creation problem — **the material already exists.** brand.md documents verified win themes, authentic scarcity mechanics, a distinct voice and named proof points. None of it reaches the searcher. The offer is excellent and undeployed.

**One finding contradicts a documented assumption and deserves saying plainly.** business.md §15 lists "price-vs-quality perception" as an open blocker depressing non-brand conversion. This audit finds the value-to-price case is actually well constructed — threshold-designed free shipping at €89 (deliberately set just under a single hoodie), a recurring buy-3-get-1 mechanic, GSM specs in every product title, 200-wash durability claims. If a perception problem exists, the evidence points to it being a **communication** gap on the landing page rather than a genuine value gap. That reframes where to spend effort.

**Two real weaknesses.** There is no *named* mechanism — no "The Stay Cold Method" — so the differentiation lives in the art rather than in an ownable process. And the dispatch promise is internally inconsistent across three surfaces, which quietly undermines an otherwise strong trust pillar.

Read Critical Issues, then Module 1. Baseline run — no prior score.

## Critical issues

None of the four WARNs is a failure, but two are worth acting on and one is worth *not* acting on.

### 1. The dispatch promise contradicts itself across three surfaces

| Surface | Claim |
|---|---|
| Site announcement bar | "Worldwide Delivery within **~1–2 business days**" |
| FAQ | Dispatch within **2–3 business days** |
| Product page (Ravenous) | **24 hours** |

Three different numbers for the same promise. And the banner wording says *delivery*, not dispatch, while the FAQ's actual delivery table reads: Germany 2–3 days, EU 4–7, UK 8, **USA 10–14 days**, Canada 3–4 weeks, Australia 4–6 weeks.

Two consequences. **Commercially:** a US buyer told "worldwide delivery within 1–2 business days" who waits two weeks is a return and a bad review waiting to happen — and this account's return rate is already ~5%, with sizing driving 54% of it. **For advertising:** business.md §10 and brand.md both already flag this as a misleading-claim risk under Google Ads policy for non-DE geos. It must not enter RSA copy or promotion extensions.

This does not fail any single diagnostic — returns, guarantee and proof are all strong — but it is the one place where the offer actively erodes its own trust pillar. **Establish a single source of truth before any copy work.**

### 2. There is no named mechanism (D05 WARN)

The differentiation is real but it lives entirely in the artwork and the artists. There is no packaged, ownable *method* — no "The Stay Cold Process", no named quality standard, no named design pipeline. The closest things are merchandising rhythms ("Thunder Drop Weeks", "drops not seasons") and a spec habit (GSM in every product title), neither of which is positioned as a mechanism.

This matters less for apparel than for services, and the artwork uniqueness carries the comparison-resistance load on its own (D04 PASS). But naming the quality standard — the GSM ladder, the 200-wash print test, the artist-credit model — would convert an implicit habit into a claimable asset.

### 3. Do NOT treat "price-vs-quality perception" as an offer problem

business.md §15 lists it as an open, documented blocker. This audit disagrees on cause. The value-to-price construction is strong:

- Free shipping threshold at **€89** — deliberately positioned just under one hoodie (€119.90) and just above two tees (€99.80). That is designed, not accidental.
- **Thunder Drop Deal #9** — buy any 3, get a 4th free. A standing AOV lever with a rotating gift.
- GSM weight in every product title (200 / 250 / 350 / 400) as a hard, checkable quality proof.
- 30-day returns above the statutory 14.
- "Limited art piece" framing that resists commodity comparison.

If buyers perceive a price-quality mismatch, the likeliest explanation given today's other findings is that **the proof never reaches them** — the landing page receiving non-brand traffic leads with a brand slogan rather than the GSM/artist/durability case. That is an LP and messaging fix, not an offer redesign.

## Recommendations

### Act now

1. **Fix the dispatch claim to one number, on every surface.** Banner, FAQ and product pages currently say three different things, and the banner says "delivery" where it means "dispatch". This is a live misleading-claim risk for non-DE geos and a return driver. → site/dev work, then feed the agreed wording to ad copy
2. **Put numbers on the scarcity.** "Only 7 left" beats "Only a few items left"; "Drop closes Friday 23:59" beats "Limited". The scarcity is genuinely real — communicating it precisely costs nothing and is the one urgency gap on the checklist. → `/offer-maker create`, Urgency pillar
3. **Extract the offer angles that already exist.** This is the bridge to the session's biggest finding. → `/offer-maker angles`

### Structural

4. **Name the quality mechanism.** The GSM ladder, the 200-wash print standard and the artist-credit model are already practised — packaging them as a named standard converts a habit into a claimable asset. → `/offer-maker create`, Uniqueness pillar
5. **Document the return-shipping policy.** Who pays is the decisive friction question in apparel and it is currently unstated. → business/ops decision
6. **Resolve the competitor domains** so `/competitor-scraper` can run against the seven named brands. Config leaves them empty on purpose; that decision is correct but blocks D16. → **Jonas**

### Do not do

7. **Do not redesign the offer.** At 97% it is the strongest asset in the account. Every FAIL in the other ten audits is upstream or downstream of it, never in it.
8. **Do not treat "price-vs-quality perception" as an offer defect** until the landing page has been fixed — see Critical issue 3.
